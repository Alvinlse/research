\begin{abstract}
On a saturated GPU cluster, utilisation and SLA compliance trade off through a rationing
decision: whose safety margin gets cut. Recent work proposes letting a reasoning LLM make that
decision. We test whether it earns its cost, by decomposition rather than by a single arm. On
the Alibaba cluster-trace-gpu-v2020 replayed as a two-sided allocation game (32 paired seeds,
two progress laws), (1) a deterministic bid/ask market wins every efficiency metric —
utilisation, useful utilisation, oracle regret — against every LLM arm at zero tokens, with a
null deadline effect; (2) the largest service-quality lever we measure is also
deterministic: ordering grants by current deadline laxity cuts violations from 23.8\% to 7.4\%
($-16.4 \pm 3.1$\sig) at zero tokens; (3) the reasoning layer earns its cost precisely on
\emph{text exceptions} the numbers cannot express — on a pre-registered suite of 81 such cases,
a debate of LLM reviewers corrects a single model's rulings (43/81 vs.\ 29/81 at an identical
call budget, one-sided exact McNemar $p=0.0007$), while the same budget spent on best-of-$N$
sampling buys nothing ($p=0.25$); and (4) the boundary is exact, not inferred: the same
escalation, run unmodified inside the simulator, is a literal no-op — zero LLM calls, zero
changes, an allocation identical to the bare market tick for tick — because public GPU traces
carry no operator free text. Invoked where no exception exists, reasoning \emph{costs} accuracy
(12/17 vs.\ the market's 16/17). We conclude that the deterministic layer should decide, and
reasoning should be gated behind a contextual trigger: a capability that costs nothing until
text arrives.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

AI and scientific workloads are bursty and heterogeneous; static batch schedulers cannot hold
both high utilisation and a low SLA-violation rate. The reason is physical: in the saturated
regime it is impossible to give every job a safety margin, so what sets service quality is the
rationing decision — \emph{whose margin gets cut} — which classical schedulers make blindly.

Recent work~\cite{llmsched} proposes a \emph{reason-then-guarantee} spine: an LLM proposes a
schedule and a fast solver repairs it to feasibility. The appeal is that an LLM can reason over
a messy multi-objective situation and generalise to unfamiliar states. This paper asks the
question that determines whether that appeal is real for rationing: \textbf{does the reasoning
layer earn its cost, and if so, on which decisions?}

Our contributions:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{The deterministic market wins efficiency at zero cost} (\S\ref{sec:market}). A
bid/ask auction over each job's real marginal useful progress beats every LLM arm on
utilisation, useful utilisation, and regret, under both progress laws, with no LLM calls — and
its deadline effect is null, so the gain is throughput, not service quality bought
elsewhere.
\item \textbf{The largest SLA lever is also deterministic} (\S\ref{sec:laxity}). Recomputing
bid urgency from normalised deadline slack every tick — least-laxity grant ordering — cuts
violations 23.8\% $\rightarrow$ 7.4\% and slowdown $17.8 \rightarrow 5.4$ at zero tokens.
Every LLM arm is flat against this floor.
\item \textbf{Reasoning earns its cost on text exceptions, and structure — not budget — is
what pays} (\S\ref{sec:text}). On a pre-registered 81-case suite of text-dependent exceptions,
debate between two reviewers corrects a single LLM's rulings at an identical call budget
(43/81 vs.\ 29/81, $p=0.0007$); best-of-$N$ at the same budget is flat ($p=0.25$); a blind
50-case confirmatory batch reproduces the effect ($p=0.0032$).
\item \textbf{The boundary is measured exactly} (\S\ref{sec:boundary}). The same escalation,
gated behind a contextual trigger and run unmodified in-sim, makes zero LLM calls and zero
changes — the allocation is identical to the bare market tick for tick — because the trace
carries no operator text. Where no exception exists, reasoning \emph{costs} accuracy (12/17
vs.\ 16/17). Gating is therefore load-bearing, not decorative.
\end{enumerate}

The governing rule throughout is \emph{the LLM reasons and explains; deterministic code
decides} — and our findings sharpen it: on numeric scenes the deterministic code should also
\emph{decide efficiently on its own}, and reasoning should be invoked only where text carries a
fact the numbers cannot.

\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\textbf{LLM-driven scheduling.} LLMsched~\cite{llmsched} repairs a single LLM's proposal with
an ILP and reports large gains over Kubernetes. We inherit the reason-then-guarantee spine but
invert its default: on numeric rationing the deterministic layer should be the proposer, not
just the repairer, and the LLM should be gated behind a contextual trigger.
\todo{2--3 more LLM-scheduling refs}

\textbf{Learning-based schedulers.} DRL schedulers (DeepRM~\cite{deeprm},
Decima~\cite{decima}) learn allocation policies but are black-box and brittle under
distribution shift. On our interface a tabular Q-learner's episode-return variance dominates
the action effect at realisable sample counts.

\textbf{Multi-agent LLM structures.} Multiagent debate~\cite{du2024debate} and
self-consistency~\cite{wang2023selfconsistency} are usually evaluated on QA benchmarks, and
LLM-as-judge protocols~\cite{zheng2023judge} inherit that setting; budget-matched controls are
rare, so a reported gain is rarely separable from the extra calls that buy it. Our
text-exception result is budget-matched by construction and includes a blind confirmatory
batch.

\textbf{Malleability and mechanism design.} Runtime elasticity is rarely deployed in
production~\cite{tarraf2024}; our partial-allocation model rests on it and we state that
dependence. Uniform-price auctions are the classical route to truthful revelation, and
automated negotiation for resource allocation is a classical subject~\cite{boan2011}, with
prediction-guided \emph{selective} negotiation explored in one-shot fair
division~\cite{croitoru2025}; our contribution is measurement in a closed loop containing
declarative LLM agents.

\caption{The gated pipeline. Every tick runs through deterministic code (grey): the auction
clears, the validator checks feasibility. Only a trigger (operator-note conflict, production

\label{fig:pipeline}

Fig.~\ref{fig:pipeline} shows the pipeline.

\textbf{Auction (default path, zero LLM).} Each epoch, demand posts a non-increasing marginal
value curve $b_{j,k} = \alpha\,\widehat{dp}_{j,k} + \beta R_{\mathrm{SLA}} +
\gamma W_{\mathrm{wait}} - \delta C_{\mathrm{resize}}$ per job per extra GPU, where
$\widehat{dp}$ is the job's real marginal useful progress under the counterfactual progress
model; supply posts a rising ask from scarcity, fragmentation, reserve pressure and arrival
pressure; the market clears where bid meets ask.

\textbf{Validation (every tick, cheap code).} Before an allocation is applied, a deterministic
check verifies feasibility, non-negative awards and known job ids. On violation the tick
concedes to the floor. On correct clearings the validator is byte-identical to the unvalidated
market across both laws and all pools (fault injection confirms it rejects when it should).

\textbf{Escalation (only on a trigger).} A reasoning arm rules only when the auction output
conflicts with an operator note, a production job arrives, a job falls behind its deadline, or
contested capacity crosses a bucket boundary. The escalation is a \emph{debate}: demand- and
supply-side reviewers read a structured decision packet, state positions, read each other, and
revise before a referee rules. An infeasible ruling is never repaired silently; it falls back
and re-fires. Reasoning is best-effort improvement, never on the critical path.

\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\textbf{Setup.} We replay contiguous windows of the 606k-job Alibaba
cluster-trace-gpu-v2020~\cite{alibaba2020}, taking arrival, duration, and GPU demand jointly
from the trace and synthesising only what the trace lacks (tier, deadline, urgency; seeded
recipe). Standard operating point: one 8-GPU node in quarter-GPU quanta, 96 jobs per window
($3\times$ load ratio — the last ratio at which every job finishes), 30\,s ticks, deadline
slack calibrated so the floor violates 11--13\% (the violation--utilisation curve is reported
across the slack regime, so no result hangs on the calibration point). \todo{reconcile

sentence} 32 seeds, windows and seeds paired across arms; \sig{} marks a 95\% CI excluding 0;

saturating law; a claim counts only if it holds under both. LLMs are local Ollama models

pipeline with ILP guarantees (our prior system, kept as a baseline arm), and a tabular

\subsection{The largest SLA lever is deterministic too}
\label{sec:laxity}

No arm above — LLM or market — keys on deadline laxity: grants were ordered by frozen bid
value. Recomputing each bid's urgency every tick from normalised slack,
$\mathrm{lax} = (d - t - \mathrm{rem})/(d - \mathrm{arr})$, mapped onto the same urgency band
so only the \emph{ordering} changes, gives least-laxity grant ordering at zero tokens:

This is the largest single service-quality effect we measure, it is invariant to the deadline
calibration (reproduced across the slack-multiplier regime), and every LLM arm is flat against
it ($|$dSLA$| \le 0.1$, all ns). The pattern across our calibration experiments is consistent:
each time the world is made more realistic or a deterministic lever is pulled, the space the
reasoning layer could occupy shrinks. What remains for it is what no number carries at all —
text.

\subsection{Where reasoning earns its cost: text exceptions}
\label{sec:text}

A deterministic mechanism cannot act on an exception whose content is \emph{text}: an operator
instruction, a declaration a note says is wrong, a crash-restart the numbers do not show. We
measure this on a pre-registered suite of hand-authored exception scenes.

\textbf{The suite} (Table~\ref{tab:suite}). Each case is a small allocation scene (median two
jobs, one supply note, 2--8 free GPUs, $\sim$36 words of prose) with a pre-registered predicate
defining the defensible ruling, authored \emph{before} any LLM was run on it. The authoring
rule is text-dependence: deleting the exception sentence leaves a scene whose numbers imply a
different, perfectly reasonable answer. \textbf{Admission required both rigid arms — a fixed
ILP over the same submissions and a rule-based floor — to fail the predicate; the 0/81 rigid
floor in Table~\ref{tab:arms} is therefore an inclusion criterion, not a finding.} What the
suite measures is the comparison \emph{among} arms that can read text. Two control categories
guard the boundary: \emph{placebo} scenes with decorative or expired prose (including a keyword
trap naming a policy the note itself retracts) and \emph{confirm} scenes whose salient prose
confirms the default ranking; the fixed-ILP arm and the deterministic market pass 17/17 and
16/17 of these, respectively. Of the 81 primary cases, 50 were authored blind to the per-case
outcomes of the earlier under-powered round, and
the harness reproduces that round's head-to-head exactly ($b{=}6$, $c{=}1$).

LLM arm): debate helps when reviewers must notice an absence, not follow a presence.

\subsection{The boundary, measured exactly}
\label{sec:boundary}

The natural objection is that an authored suite proves little about a real workload. We
therefore ran \emph{this exact escalation} — unmodified, LLM enabled — inside the simulator,
gated behind the \S\ref{sec:arch} trigger, at the standard operating point (n=32 paired

The two arms are identical on every metric, every paired vs.-floor delta, and every surviving
Holm $p$-value, in both laws — the same allocation tick for tick. The trigger genuinely fired
(\textbf{5{,}494 escalations} under amdahl and \textbf{5{,}517} under sat, across the 32
seeds); each escalation entered the correction pipeline, found no job carrying text, and
returned, making
\textbf{zero LLM calls and zero changes}. The reason is structural: public GPU traces are scrubbed of operator free text
(it is PII), so the channel this capability acts on is absent by construction — a fact we
verified across the public trace landscape rather than synthesising notes into a trace that
has none.

This is the boundary stated exactly rather than inferred: the mechanism that corrects 43/81
text-exception rulings is a literal no-op where no text exists. It cannot help — and, the part
that matters for deployment, it cannot hurt or cost anything either. Combined with the
specificity cost above (12/17 vs.\ 16/17), the design conclusion is symmetric: reasoning must
be gated \emph{on} when text arrives and gated \emph{off} when it does not, and both gates are
now measured.

\section{Threats to Validity}
\label{sec:threats}

Tier, deadline, and urgency are a synthetic recipe over a real trace; the replay world never
preempts base allocations, so we make no free-malleability claims. That rigidity also bounds
what \emph{any} efficiency claim here can demonstrate, and we can now say by how much. When no
job can be resized, the GPU-seconds a workload consumes are fixed: a scheduler chooses only
\emph{when} work runs, never how much of it there is, so utilisation is close to a conserved
quantity. Replaying the MIT Supercloud GPU trace through a malleability-aware batch simulator
\cite{elastisim} in the same rigid mode, five schedulers on one window differed by at most
$1.3$ points of occupancy, and the idle capacity any ordering rule could still recover was
$0.2\%$. Claim~(1) is therefore established in a regime where the achievable efficiency spread
is structurally small; it should be read as ``the market is not beaten on efficiency here,''
not as evidence that reasoning cannot improve efficiency on an elastic workload. The text-exception suite is
authored, not sampled from operations, and its ground-truth predicates are
single-annotator \todo{replace with rater-agreement number}; the rigid-arm floor is an
inclusion criterion by design. The headline debate result is one model family at one scale
\todo{update if gemma replication lands}. SLA levels depend on a calibrated slack multiplier;
contrasts are therefore reported across the calibration regime, and the operating point is one
where every job finishes. LLM latency is hidden behind wait time by the gate in the design,
but evaluation runs on simulation clock.

\section{Conclusion}

The division of labour ``the LLM reasons and explains; deterministic code decides'' is not a
slogan but the empirically optimal split — sharpened. On numeric GPU rationing the
deterministic layer should also decide \emph{efficiently on its own}: the market wins every
efficiency metric at zero tokens, and the largest SLA lever is a deterministic ordering rule.
The reasoning layer earns its cost precisely on the exceptions numbers cannot express — text —
where structured debate corrects a single model's unreliable rulings at matched budget, and a
gated architecture delivers that capability at exactly zero cost where the text is absent. The
open frontier is a workload that actually carries operator text, on which the
efficiency-neutral capability should convert into a measurable service-quality gain.

A second frontier is \emph{elasticity}, and it is the one our own negative efficiency result
argues for. AI training and inference jobs are not rigid: they scale across devices, so a
scheduler on such a workload decides not only which job runs but how large it is. That second
decision does not exist in any of the replays above, which is precisely why efficiency is
near-conserved in them. Restoring it changes the problem rather than the tuning: on a
moldable replay of Supercloud under Amdahl scaling, the sizing rule alone moves mean waiting
time by an order of magnitude, and the rule that maximises occupancy is the worst of the three
we tried --- it converts the cluster into busy-ness without converting it into completed work.
We regard sizing under uncertainty, not ordering, as the decision on which a reasoning layer
should next be asked to earn its cost \todo{fold in the powered elastic run when it lands;
current elastic numbers are a single window and directional only}.
